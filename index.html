Score figures with the manuscript, not pixels alone.

A peer-review-oriented benchmark and fine-tuned scorer that binds each published figure to its caption, citing paragraphs, and paper context—predicting Clarity, Relevance, Informativeness, and Structure.

The SciFigAlign Benchmark

Built from peer-reviewed CS conference PDFs (ICLR / NeurIPS / ICML). Each figure is index-bound to manuscript evidence and rated on a 1–5 peer-review rubric—not generic IQA aesthetics.

Rubric dimensions
Clarity (CL)Print-scale legibility, contrast, labels, legends.
Relevance (RE)Fit to caption, citing text, and paper narrative.
Informativeness (IN)Scientific content density for the claimed result.
Structure (ST)Panel hierarchy and coherent reading path.

Manuscript-Grounded Scoring

SciFigAlign fine-tunes CLIP Vision and SciBERT end-to-end with per-modality cross-attention and CubeMLP fusion, jointly optimizing SmoothL1 regression and within-paper ranking.

  • Index-resolved citing context Recover “Figure k / Fig. k” paragraphs instead of layout-only OCR neighborhoods.
  • Per-modality alignment Caption, cites, abstract, and metadata each query visual patches separately.
  • Within-paper ranking hinge Preserves relative order among figures from the same manuscript under mid–high score concentration.
  • Auditable signals Exportable attention maps and CubeMLP modality weights for inspection.

Corpus Statistics

From 12,492 raw crops to 3,857 labeled figures with paper-level splits. Architecture / Other / Qualitative mix, score density around mean 3.54, and human + GPT-4o assisted labels under one rubric.

Why this benchmark?

Natural IQA cannot read claims. CLIP-style similarity is too coarse for peer-review rubrics. Generation faithfulness targets synthetic images. Zero-shot VLM judges are hard to calibrate. SciFigAlign scores published figures with manuscript evidence and learned ranking.
